Does the public feed move markets?

We built Feed Reader to turn unstructured public text into risk-capped trades. Before trusting any signal, we tested the premise end to end: which public events actually move equity prices and volatility, where the move is already priced in, and whether an LLM has a real edge. This note is what the data said.

Study window Jun 20 – Oct 19 2026 · 1,663 posts classified by Bedrock nova-lite · price & volatility from intraday and daily bars · SEC 8-K via Apify/EDGAR · all figures from the analysis scripts in demo/.

What we concluded

  • TWEETS · NOPublic posts (incl. @realDonaldTrump) do not move volatility or price. After removing hour-of-day, the VIX regime, the market factor and each name's own drift, the effect is zero (p > 0.3).
  • FOMC · PRICED INFOMC spikes volatility ×2.6–5.5 at the announcement hour, but it's anticipated: options carry the premium beforehand, direction is a coin flip. A risk event, not a signal.
  • 8-K · PRICED INMaterial filings move single names hard, but the move is a pre-market gap off the newswire. EDGAR and news feeds lag it by hours. No reactable window for a spot agent.
  • READ-THROUGH · EDGEThe model's cross-entity read-through inference shows a measured +1.5% / 3-day abnormal return (p≈0.001), robust to every control and placebo we ran. Direct keyword mentions show nothing.

02

Tweets don't move volatility

We compared volatility in hours containing a market-relevant post against quiet hours, after normalizing out the intraday U-shape and the day's VIX regime, then removing the market factor and each name's own drift. Across SPY and the big single names the effect sits on zero.

03

What does move volatility — and why it's already priced

The real driver is scheduled macro. On the two FOMC announcement days in the window, volatility exploded in the 2:00pm ET hour — up to ×5.5 normal for SPY — then crushed the next day (VIX 20.7 → 17.1). But it's anticipated: the options term structure prices the FOMC day at roughly twice a normal day in advance, and the direction is a coin flip.

04

Material filings move — but the feed is too late

SEC 8-Ks do move single names, often violently. Across 185 filings the median was a non-event (2.8% move, ~1× normal vol), but a tail of deal / financing / control filings moved 30–150%.

The catch: we measured the reaction to the minute. The big move is a pre-market gap off the primary press-wire release. The EDGAR 8-K and the news recap we can cheaply read arrive hours later and produce ~0 reaction. ManpowerGroup is the clean example.

05

The edge: read-through inference

The one place reasoning beats latency is cross-entity read-through: an event about company A whose material implication is to an allowlisted name B. The microsecond algos price the direct subject instantly; the spillover takes judgment to connect, and it pays over a few days.

Read-through · worked

Tariffs → NVDA

"They have also charged our great farmers…massive amounts of Tariffs."

Model: tariffs hit tech → NVDA long. +9.4% over 3 days.
Read-through · worked

Rates → NVDA

"Interest Rates in the United States should be 1%, or less…"

Model: lower rates lift tech multiples → NVDA long. +6.3% over 3 days.

What it is not: proven, deployable alpha. It lives in one four-month window and one corpus; the gate and horizon were chosen after seeing results; costs and slippage aren't modeled. The individual links look loose — the statistical edge is across the set, not in any single call. The honest next step is a pre-registered, out-of-sample forward test before a cent rides on it.

06

Decisions for the build

The data settles the positioning. Feed Reader is not a speed play and shouldn't claim to be — material news is priced in a gap before any feed we can read reflects it.

What we're shipping

  • BRAINLead with read-through interpretation — the model connecting an event to an allowlisted name is where the measured edge is, and it's genuinely beyond a keyword wrapper.
  • HORIZONTrade the multi-day drift, entering after the open. Skip the first-second pop we can't win.
  • RISKSince there's no timing edge, the on-chain mandate does the work: confidence gate, drawdown cap, allowlist, and an FOMC-window size-down. Bounded, explainable, regulator-defensible.
  • AVOIDNo "reacts fast to news" claim; no trading into the FOMC print; no betting direction on scheduled events.

In one line: we don't out-run the market on news — we interpret unstructured signals and let the protocol enforce the risk, provably, on-chain.

Method & data.
  • Posts: Truth Social @realDonaldTrump via Apify, cleaned and deduped; classification by Bedrock eu.amazon.nova-lite-v1:0, temperature 0.
  • Volatility test: hourly range normalized by hour-of-day × VIX regime; within-day fixed effect; market-model (beta) residual for single names; permutation + bootstrap inference.
  • Read-through backtest: signed abnormal return (beta-adjusted, own-drift removed), entry at the close after the post, +3d exit; week-block bootstrap, non-overlap subset, date split, random-date placebo (3,000 draws).
  • 8-K: nexgendata/EDGAR via Apify, joined to Yahoo intraday/daily; acceptance timestamps from SEC submissions.
  • Illustrative research, synthetic/testnet context — not investment advice. Figures are in-sample; treat the read-through edge as a hypothesis pending out-of-sample confirmation.
