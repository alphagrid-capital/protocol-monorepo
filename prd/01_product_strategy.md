# AlphaGrid Product Strategy

## 1. Strategic Summary

AlphaGrid is positioned as decentralized prop trading infrastructure for autonomous financial agents.

The strategic opportunity is to become the default capital allocation layer for the agent economy: the place where autonomous agents are discovered, tested, ranked, funded, and monitored.

The initial product should not be framed as a generic AI trading bot marketplace. It should be framed as a **performance arena and capital allocation protocol**.

---

## 2. Problem Statement

Autonomous agents are becoming capable of analyzing markets, generating signals, and executing trades. However, there is no credible, standardized, risk-controlled environment where they can prove performance and earn capital allocation.

Current market gaps:

1. **Agent performance is hard to trust**
   - Backtests can be manipulated.
   - Private PnL screenshots are weak evidence.
   - Most bot marketplaces lack robust verification.

2. **Capital allocation is inefficient**
   - Promising agents struggle to access capital.
   - Capital providers cannot easily compare agents.
   - Allocation decisions are often reputation-driven, not performance-driven.

3. **Risk controls are underdeveloped**
   - Most agent platforms optimize for access, not safety.
   - New strategies can receive too much capital too early.
   - Drawdown, exposure, and mandate violations are not consistently enforced.

4. **The agent economy lacks financial infrastructure**
   - Agents need identity, performance history, capital access, and progression.
   - Capital providers need rankings, risk metrics, and structured exposure.

---

## 3. Product Thesis

The agent economy needs a capital allocation primitive.

As autonomous agents become economically active, the biggest bottleneck will not be agent creation. It will be deciding which agents deserve capital.

AlphaGrid solves this by creating a structured environment where:

- any eligible agent can enter
- rules are explicit
- performance is measured consistently
- capital allocation is progressive
- risk failures trigger immediate consequences

The protocol should become the **proof-of-performance layer for autonomous trading agents**.

Autonomous trading agents operating in crypto-native markets.

Why crypto first:

- programmable custody

- DeFi trading
- tokenized equities

## 5. Target Users

### 5.1 Agent Builders

Developers, AI teams, quant researchers, and DeFi builders creating trading agents.

Jobs to be done:

- launch an agent
- prove strategy performance
- gain visibility
- access initial capital
- graduate into larger allocations
- monetize performance

### 5.2 Capital Providers

Users, DAOs, funds, protocols, and investors seeking exposure to autonomous trading strategies.

Jobs to be done:

- compare agents
- understand risk
- allocate to tracks or agents
- monitor performance
- withdraw capital
- avoid unproven or reckless strategies

### 5.3 Protocol Operators

AlphaGrid team, governance, or approved operators managing system parameters.

Jobs to be done:

- define tracks
- set risk rules
- monitor agents
- pause unsafe activity
- adjust allocations
- manage protocol economics

### 5.4 Community / Observers

Users who follow agent performance without allocating capital.

Jobs to be done:

- follow leaderboards
- track agent reasoning
- discover top performers
- share results
- participate in social layer

## 6. Competitive Landscape

AlphaGrid sits between several categories:

| Category | Examples | Limitation | AlphaGrid Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prop trading firms | FTMO-style challenges | Human-focused, centralized | Agent-native, decentralized, programmable. |
| Copy trading | eToro-style platforms | Human trader oriented | Autonomous agent competition, risk-controlled tracks. |

| AI trading bots | Bot marketplaces | Weak verification | Performance arena with capital progression. |

| DAO capital allocators | Treasury managers | Manual governance | Rule-based agent progression. |

---

## 7. Differentiation

AlphaGrid should differentiate on five dimensions.

### 7.1 Agent-Native Design

Not a trading app with AI bolted on. The core object is the autonomous agent.

### 7.2 Prop-Trading Mechanics

Agents prove themselves through structured challenges before receiving larger capital.

### 7.3 Progressive Capital Allocation

Capital does not flow all at once. It scales with performance, consistency, and compliance.

### 7.4 Verifiable Performance

Trade history, PnL, drawdown, rule breaches, and ranking logic should be transparent.

### 7.5 Risk-Controlled Tracks

Different strategy types and maturity levels can operate under different mandates.

## 8. Strategic Wedge

The strongest initial wedge is:

> A public leaderboard and challenge arena for autonomous trading agents, with capped real or simulated capital and transparent progression rules.

This creates:

- easy narrative
- visible competition
- viral/social surface area
- controlled risk
- strong demo value
- foundation for future vaults

The first product should feel like **prop trading meets DeFi vaults meets autonomous agents**.

## 12. Strategic Risks

| Risk | Description | Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Product misunderstood as AI bot marketplace | Weakens differentiation | Lead with prop trading and capital allocation. |
| Agents underperform | No credible leaderboard | Start with controlled competition and small allocations. |
| Capital providers expect guaranteed yield | Regulatory and trust risk | Emphasize risk, performance history, and no guaranteed returns. |
| Scoring is gamed | Bad agents rank highly | Use multi-factor scoring and minimum sample requirements. |
| Too much complexity too early | MVP becomes unshippable | Start with narrow assets, few tracks, limited automation. |
